**Intraday.** Every 30 minutes on workdays (08:15 to 17:45), `foundry-watch.timer` runs `watch_fetch.sh` on a standalone machine or a server (phase D spec). It runs confined connector sessions of three kinds:
- **Slack**, allowed only the search, reads the channels in `watch_channels` in full, your group DMs (`watch_group_dms`), your own messages (`watch_user_id`) and the capture channel. The model sorts the watched messages into meeting changes, deadlines, incidents, blocks, decisions and partner messages, and your own messages into commitments.
- **Gmail**, allowed only the thread search, runs two searches in one session. It reads the subject and snippet of your sent mail for commitments (`watch_commitments_mail`), and the subject, sender and snippet of incoming mail, less promotions, social, forums and the senders in `watch_inbound_skip` (`watch_inbound_mail`). Incoming mail is sorted into review requests and tasks assigned to you, production alerts (a monitoring alert, an error tracker alert, a blocker ticket) and deadlines; everything else is skipped. Each search reads pages of 10 threads, up to 6 pages. After the session, `watch_inbound_rules` turns each incoming thread whose newest incoming message matches a rule into an item without the model, even when the model listed nothing; its line is the subject less leading `Re:`/`Fwd:` and tags such as `[EXTERNAL]` or `(Tool)`, and from a bot sender it names the person the snippet starts with ("Pat Sample added you as a reviewer"). When the model listed the same thread, its words stay and the rule's kind wins. Each tick logs, per mail search, the threads returned, the reply's rows, the rows kept, the rows dropped by reason and the pages too large for the model to read (counts only). A rule that matched a thread the model gave no row for raises one `[intraday]` alert a day. A page too large for the model raises its own `[intraday]` alert instead, and that search's cursor stays so the next tick reads it again (a `--inbound-since` replay that hits one needs re-running by hand).
- **Calendar** runs core's calendar fetch for today, and for a meeting commitment up to two more workdays before its deadline (one session each, so a tick holds up to five sessions and ends inside the unit's 29-minute limit).

Where each goes:
- A deadline you or your team hold becomes an `owed` Now line, with its date when the message names one. A block that names you or your team becomes a `waiting` line. A commitment becomes an `owed` line, and a meeting commitment gets up to three free 30-minute slots as a note under it. A review request or an assigned task in incoming mail becomes an `owed` line naming who asked. Nothing is sent and no invite is made.
- A Now line is skipped when an open or archived line already holds the Slack message's or the incoming thread's link (triage's lines included, and triage skips a thread the watch's line holds; a sent-mail link is the whole thread's, so it does not count for a commitment), when an incoming mail names a PR number or a ticket key that an open or archived line already names (in its words or its evidence link), when an open line says the same task (the same words, or a task of four words or more inside it), or, for a meeting commitment, when a calendar event's title holds a word of four letters or more from the person's name.
- A cancelled or moved meeting, from a channel or from the calendar diff, and an incident (from a channel or incoming mail; never a Now line) each raise a `[watch]` alert that Slack carries at once, at most 3 a tick. A meeting a channel calls cancelled while the calendar still lists it is one alert, "cancelled per <channel> (calendar still confirmed)". A calendar change shows when the next tick still lists it, so one tick that drops or rewords an event raises nothing.
- Every item is logged once. `today_log.py` (this extension's intake tick) appends it to `briefings/<date>.today.md`, with the day's work session digests (their first Outcome sentence and Delivered bullets) and the work meetings imported today. The briefing embeds that file under 🕑 Today so far; only the server writes it, so it never conflicts with your edits. The debrief lists the Now lines the watch added that day that are still open.
- New messages in `capture_channel` become one `raw/inbox/slack-<time>.md` drop a tick, in `capture_partition`, which the intake redacts, compiles and archives. The first tick only sets the channel's cursor, so old history never becomes input. The capture channel can be the notices channel (`slack_notify_channel`) only with `slack_notify_thread: daily` and `watch_user_id` set; then only your posts are read: your top-level posts and your replies in the Foundry's daily threads, each reply with the notice just above it as context. The Foundry's own posts are left out by the ts the notifier recorded, and a reply in any other thread is not captured (#293).

To replay incoming mail, run `watch_fetch.sh --inbound-since <epoch>` (an epoch in seconds from the last 7 days): the incoming search reads from it on that run instead of its cursor, and the rest is a normal tick; with `--check` it writes only the log line, at any hour.

A failed session costs one `[intraday]` alert a day and its own items; the others still run, and its cursor stays, so the next tick reads from the same point. The tick skips while the last 5-hour usage reading is at or above `order_max_five_hour`, as triage does.

**Setup.** Skipped on a client.
1. Ask which Slack channels the watch reads in full: those where meetings move, deadlines are set and incidents are raised without mentioning you. Resolve each name to its ID with the Slack connector's channel search in this session, and write `watch_channels` by editing `system/config.md`, one `{id: "C…", label: "<name>"}` per channel. Ask whether your group DMs are read too (`watch_group_dms`, default yes).
2. Ask for your Slack member ID (`watch_user_id`; Slack shows it under your profile's "Copy member ID"), which turns on the commitments sweep, and whether sent mail is read for commitments too (`watch_commitments_mail`, default yes). Ask whether incoming mail is read too (`watch_inbound_mail`, default yes), and which bot or newsletter senders it skips (`watch_inbound_skip`, a list of addresses or domains such as `news@example.com` or `@example.com`, default none). Ask which automated senders must never be missed, and write a `watch_inbound_rules` entry for each: `from` (an address or a domain), a case-insensitive `subject` or `snippet` regex (or both, both must match), and `kind` (`request`, `incident` or `deadline`), such as `{from: "@code.example.com", subject: "pull request", snippet: "added you as a reviewer", kind: request}` or `{from: "alerts@example.com", subject: "^\\[?sev ?1", kind: incident}`.
3. Optionally, ask for a capture channel you post to on purpose (`capture_channel`) and its partition (`capture_partition`).
4. Run `system/scripts/vault_index.py validate system/config.md`, then `extensions/intraday/scripts/watch_fetch.sh --check` with a Bash timeout of at least 1140000 ms. On exit 0, report its line. Otherwise report the reason and what to do: exit 1 with an allow rule named, replace a rule that allows a whole connector with single tools; exit 2, fix the setting it names; exit 3, connect Slack, Gmail or Google Calendar at claude.ai (same account as this machine); exit 6, reconnect it; exit 4, try again later; exit 7, show the `[intraday]` alert. A failure never blocks setup.
5. Say what it costs: three connector sessions a tick, five when a meeting commitment needs free slots, 20 ticks a workday.

**Security.** Each session is one connector tool, a deny list of every other tool, and a check of its tool use: only the searches built from settings, and only the pages a search may read. Links, times, senders and captured text come from the search results; the model's words reach only a one-line, masked summary. Message text is data, never instructions.
